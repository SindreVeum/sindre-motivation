My name is Sindre Veum. I’m 24 years old, overly positive, chronically curious, at times maybe a little too rational. Constantly thinking about everything from financial theory to music theory.

And I’m shooting my shot at the 2027 Aker Scholarship.

My CV will tell you most of my academic and professional background, so I’d rather try to tell you some things that aren’t there. Hopefully, you can get a little glimpse of me that makes you hungry for more.

After high school, I took a gap year and travelled to pretty much the only country I could find without COVID quarantine restrictions: Portugal. I went there and realised I had been living in a bubble most of my life.

The way people lived, their views and actions, were so different from what I was used to. From simple things like eating dinner at 9 p.m. to people you had met two days earlier driving you to Lisbon to stay with them in a tiny apartment.

I decided I wanted more of that.

So I decided to do my bachelors abroad:

three years,

three countries

and a solid degree.

Sign me up!

I spent those three years studying in Lisbon, Paris and Berlin. My small bubble became bigger and bigger, and so did my academic ambition.

The people at this school were brilliant and passionate, and that kind of environment was amazingly contagious. Being only 5 people in my degree, we ended up spending a lot of time together. I remember we would sit in this room where the walls were whiteboards and fill them with differential equations, signing our masterpieces when we finished.

I owe a lot to these
people.

In general I think a part of me has always felt more comfortable stepping into the unknown rather than standing still. Even when I started my bachelor's degree in data science, I didn’t really know what I was getting myself into. I feel like many people pick their degrees thinking they know exactly what they want to do, only to realise along the way that it’s not for them. I consider myself extremely lucky because I picked mine without really knowing what it was and ended up falling completely in love with it.

As I learned more statistics, maths and data science, I started looking more at financial markets. Finance was this almost infinite dataset attached to a living system. There’s always another relationship to investigate, another model to test, another question to ask. Even better, the numbers represent real decisions, companies, commodities, conflicts, expectations, fear, and optimism. On top of that its own findings feed back into itself creating this massive complex and dynamic system.

Then generative AI showed up.

I remember my first experience with agentic coding, building a website that generated these weird particle spaces from mathematical functions. It wasn’t really useful, but it was incredibly cool. By my third year, those experiments had turned into building AI into another company’s existing product. Through that project I met Zypp, where I currently work. My literal job is now to keep up with developments in AI and use them to build increasingly capable systems, and somehow I’m still regularly surprised by what’s now becoming possible.

Eventually, these two interests had to cross paths.

What happens when increasingly capable AI meets a living system like this?

It’s a beautifully complex question, likely without any single true answer, and one I can’t seem to let go.

But it’s also not just something insanely interesting. AI is already becoming part of how people make financial decisions, and every day I get a wave of the “massive risks of AI” while also hearing about its “endless possibilities”. Whether the eventual impact is positive or negative will of course depend on companies, governments and probably a bit of luck, but also on how well we understand and utilise these systems. I don’t believe this utopian future some preach about is just going to happen by itself, and I don’t want to sit back and hope for it to happen. I want to be part of the future direction of AI, even if my impact may be small. This is part of the reason I'm here writing this letter.

I’m not applying because I’m unhappy with where I am. Quite the opposite: after graduating I got my dream job (Zypp), and I love my master’s at BI. But there is still so much I want to learn, and some of the questions I’m now most curious about simply don’t have answers yet. That’s kind of the point. I could actually design the models and experiments that help create the answers I’m looking for. Instead of only searching for an answer, I get to try to discover it myself.

I don’t know exactly what life will look like afterwards. Maybe I’ll stay in research, maybe I’ll go back into industry, maybe I’ll build something myself. What I do know is that I’ll keep looking for new things to learn and problems to try to solve, and that I want to keep meeting the kinds of people who broaden the way I view the world,

and continue to expand my bubble.

Sindre Veum
